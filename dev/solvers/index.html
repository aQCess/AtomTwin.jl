Solvers and accuracy

play compiles a System and a Sequence into a simulation job and runs it with one of three quantum solvers, each with a semiclassical twin that also moves the atoms:

statejumpssolverwith motion
state vectornoneSchrödinger (tdse)tdse_semiclassical
state vectoradd_decay!, add_dephasing!wavefunction Monte Carlo (wfmc), one trajectory per shotwfmc_semiclassical
density matrix (density_matrix = true)anyLindblad master equation (qme)qme_semiclassical

With no initial_state, only the classical motion is integrated (newton).

Time grid and tolerance

dt is the output grid, and the largest step a solver takes. The solvers divide it further where their error control needs to; they never take a larger step.

  • Sequence(dt) records every dt (thinned by downsample).
  • Sequence(; tol) with no dt records one sample at the end of each instruction, or more often if the Hamiltonian requires it.
  • tol (default 1e-6) is the local error target per step.

What tol controls depends on the solver:

  • Schrödinger and MCWF. Each step is a Chebyshev expansion of exp(-iH dt), exact to machine precision for a Hamiltonian that is constant over the step, at any step length. A time-dependent drive (a shaped Pulse, a ramp, a move) is read once per step, at its midpoint, so dt must resolve it; compile warns when it may not.
  • Master equation. Each step is divided into Strang-split sub-steps until a step-doubling estimate of the error is below tol; drives are read at every sub-step, and the dissipator is an exact, trace-preserving channel. Here tol controls the result.

The Taylor propagator is available as compile(…; integrator = AtomTwin.Dynamiq.Taylor(order)); it is stable only for ‖H‖·dt < 2√2 and is not supported by the MCWF solver.

Quantum jumps

The MCWF solver places each quantum jump at the time the norm of the conditional state crosses its random threshold. The Chebyshev vectors of a step do not depend on the step length, so the state anywhere inside the step costs no further matrix-vector products, and the crossing time is found on it directly. Any number of jumps may fall in one step; a constant Hamiltonian costs one Chebyshev step per sample plus one propagation per jump. A PhotoDetectorSpec bound to a decay counts its jumps.

Shots run in parallel over Julia's threads. Pass rng = MersenneTwister(seed) (or any generator) to play for reproducible trajectories.

Atomic motion

Atoms move by default; play(…; frozen = true) holds them at their initial positions. A run in which nothing can move takes the frozen solver automatically. Motion is semiclassical: each atom has a classical position and velocity, integrated by velocity Verlet around the quantum step, and is subject to

  • the dipole force of every trapping beam, from each level's polarizability weighted by its population (and, for the tensor light shift, by the coherences it mixes);
  • radiation pressure of plane-wave couplings (add_coupling!(…; beam = PlanarBeam(…))): the Ehrenfest force of the plane-wave phase, integrated over the solver steps. In MCWF the momentum follows the detection record: each emitted photon was absorbed from the beam that excited the atom;
  • emission recoil of decays given a wavelength, add_decay!(…; λ), along the transition dipole's emission pattern when dipole is given.

Motional quantisation is not modelled: results are accurate while k_B T ≫ ħω_trap.

Trap light shifts

A beam passed to System light-shifts every level with a polarizability at its wavelength, by U = −α I/(2c ε₀), and pushes the atom with the same potential. Polarizabilities come from the species' models, matched by term symbol (l"3P1"), or from polarizabilities = Dict(term => model) on the atom constructor. For levels with angular momentum the tensor part is included in full, defined against the system's quantization axis (add_quantization_axis!, default ẑ): it mixes sublevels whenever the trap polarization is not along that axis. add_light_shift! replaces the automatic shift on chosen levels, to reference it to one level or to switch it with On/Off.

Solver entry points

AtomTwin.Dynamiq.tdse — Function
tdse(psi, H, tspan; kwargs...)

Schrödinger solver: evolves the state vector psi over the equidistant grid tspan under the Hamiltonian terms H, with frozen atoms.

AtomTwin.Dynamiq.newton — Function
newton(atoms, tspan; beams, modifiers, detectors)

Purely classical motion of atoms over the equidistant grid tspan. The force sweep is parallelised with @batch when length(atoms) > 2.

AtomTwin.Dynamiq.suggested_dt — Function
suggested_dt(terms, tol, order) -> Float64

Time step giving a local error of about tol, derived from the Hamiltonian rather than supplied by the user. Returns Inf when there is no Hamiltonian, so the caller must fall back to the instruction duration.

dt = θ_max / ‖H‖, with θ_max from propagator_theta for the given integrator and ‖H‖ a gershgorin_bound upper bound – so the step is conservative and the true error at most the target.
